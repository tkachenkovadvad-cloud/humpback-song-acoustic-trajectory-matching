SEQUENTIAL WORKFLOW FROM AUDIO TO ACOUSTIC TRAJECTORY
======================================================

This section describes the processing sequence implemented in
acoustic_trajectory_v1_3.py. It is intended as a compact explanation of how
the original waveform is converted into a shared three-dimensional acoustic
trajectory and how acoustic-change nodes are detected.

The complete process has two related branches:

    1. construction of the shared XYZ acoustic trajectory;
    2. detection of natural acoustic-change nodes within each signal.

Both branches begin from the same 29-dimensional frame-level acoustic
representation.


1. OVERVIEW
-----------

The main trajectory pipeline is:

    +-------------+
    |  INPUT WAV  |
    +-------------+
           |
           v
    +-------------+
    |    STFT     |
    +-------------+
           |
           v
    +--------------------------+
    | 29 FEATURES PER FRAME    |
    +--------------------------+
           |
           v
    +--------------------------+
    | SHARED ROBUST SCALING    |
    | all WAVs pooled together |
    +--------------------------+
           |
           v
    +-------------+
    |     PCA     |
    | 29 -> <=10  |
    +-------------+
           |
           v
    +-----------------------+
    | ROBUST SCALE PCA      |
    +-----------------------+
           |
           v
    +-------------+
    |    UMAP     |
    | <=10 -> 3   |
    +-------------+
           |
           v
    +-------------+
    |   X, Y, Z   |
    +-------------+
           |
           v
    +-----------------------+
    | TEMPORAL SMOOTHING    |
    +-----------------------+
           |
           v
    +-----------------------+
    | ACOUSTIC TRAJECTORY   |
    +-----------------------+


2. STEP 1 - FRAME THE AUDIO
---------------------------

Each WAV is converted from a waveform into a sequence of short overlapping
time-frequency frames using the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT).

Current parameters are:

    FFT length       = 8192 samples
    window length    = 8192 samples
    hop length       = 1536 samples
    window           = Hann
    analysis band    = 20-8000 Hz

At a sampling rate of 192 kHz, the hop corresponds to:

    1536 / 192000 = 0.008 s = 8 ms

Thus, the acoustic state of the signal is evaluated approximately every
8 ms.


3. STEP 2 - DESCRIBE EACH FRAME WITH 29 FEATURES
------------------------------------------------

For every frame, the pipeline constructs one vector:

    F(t) in R^29

containing:

     1  RMS_dB
     2  spectral centroid
     3  spectral bandwidth
     4  85% spectral rolloff
     5  spectral flatness
     6  spectral entropy
     7  spectral flux
     8  pitch / F0
     9  voiced probability
    10-29  MFCC_01 ... MFCC_20

These features represent complementary properties of the frame:

    amplitude             -> RMS_dB

    spectral position     -> centroid
    spectral spread       -> bandwidth
    upper spectral extent -> rolloff

    spectral organization -> flatness + entropy

    temporal change       -> spectral flux

    periodicity           -> pitch + voiced probability

    spectral-envelope
    shape                 -> 20 MFCCs

The result for one WAV is therefore not one feature vector, but a time-ordered
matrix:

                   29 FEATURES
              <------------------>

    frame 1   [ . . . . . . . . ]
    frame 2   [ . . . . . . . . ]
    frame 3   [ . . . . . . . . ]
       .               .
       .               .
    frame T   [ . . . . . . . . ]

                 |
                 v

              X_WAV
           shape = T x 29


4. STEP 3 - POOL THE SIGNALS INTO ONE SHARED SPACE
--------------------------------------------------

If several WAVs are being compared, their feature matrices are stacked:

    WAV 1 : T1 x 29
    WAV 2 : T2 x 29
    WAV 3 : T3 x 29
             .
             .
             .

              |
              v

    +----------------------+
    | ALL FRAMES x 29      |
    | FROM ALL INPUT WAVs  |
    +----------------------+

This step is fundamental.

The signals are not independently converted into unrelated XYZ spaces.
Instead, one common transformation is learned from the pooled acoustic data.

Consequently, the resulting trajectories occupy the same fitted coordinate
system and can be compared geometrically.


5. STEP 4 - NORMALIZE THE 29 FEATURES
-------------------------------------

The 29 features have different units and numerical ranges. For example,
frequency variables are measured in Hz, voiced probability is approximately
0-1, RMS is expressed in relative dB, and MFCCs have their own numerical
scale.

The pooled feature matrix is therefore transformed with:

    RobustScaler(quantile_range=(10,90))

For each feature separately:

                       value - median
    scaled value = -------------------------
                         Q90 - Q10

The result is then clipped to:

    -8 <= scaled value <= +8

This prevents the geometry from being dominated simply by units or by a small
number of extreme frames.

The important point is:

    BEFORE SCALING

        2000 Hz and 0.5 probability
        have completely different numerical scales.

    AFTER SCALING

        every feature is represented relative to
        its own distribution in the pooled dataset.


6. STEP 5 - COMPRESS 29 FEATURES WITH PCA
-----------------------------------------

The scaled 29-dimensional representation is reduced with Principal Component
Analysis:

    29 acoustic dimensions
              |
              v
        +-----------+
        |    PCA    |
        +-----------+
              |
              v
       <=10 dimensions

PCA combines correlated input variables into a smaller set of orthogonal
components.

This means that the pipeline does NOT assign one original acoustic variable
to one final coordinate.

Instead:

    centroid -----\
    bandwidth -----\
    pitch ----------\
    entropy ---------> PCA components
    MFCCs -----------/
    RMS ------------/
    etc. -----------/

All 29 features can therefore contribute to the reduced representation.

The PCA coordinates are then robust-scaled again and clipped to [-8,+8].

This second scaling prevents the first PCA components from dominating the
next distance-based stage simply because they have greater variance.


7. STEP 6 - PROJECT THE PCA REPRESENTATION INTO XYZ
---------------------------------------------------

The scaled PCA representation is passed to UMAP:

    <=10 PCA dimensions
             |
             v
       +-----------+
       |   UMAP    |
       +-----------+
             |
             v
        3 dimensions
             |
             v
         X   Y   Z

Current main parameters are:

    n_components = 3
    n_neighbors  <= 25
    min_dist     = 0.05
    metric       = Euclidean

Each original audio frame now has one three-dimensional position:

    frame t -> [X(t), Y(t), Z(t)]

X, Y and Z are dimensionless embedding coordinates.

They must NOT be interpreted individually as:

    X = pitch
    Y = frequency
    Z = amplitude

The acoustic information has already been combined by PCA and UMAP.

Instead, the XYZ position represents the multivariate acoustic state of the
frame in the shared space.


8. STEP 7 - CONNECT THE FRAMES THROUGH TIME
-------------------------------------------

The XYZ points retain their original chronological order:

    t1       t2       t3       t4               tN

     o ------ o ------ o ------ o ---- ... ----- o
     |        |        |        |                 |
    XYZ1     XYZ2     XYZ3     XYZ4              XYZN

This ordered path is the acoustic trajectory.

In mathematical form:

    trajectory =
        {
            [X(1), Y(1), Z(1)],
            [X(2), Y(2), Z(2)],
            ...
            [X(T), Y(T), Z(T)]
        }

The XYZ coordinates are lightly smoothed through time after projection to
reduce small frame-to-frame jitter.

Therefore, the final trajectory represents:

    WHERE the acoustic state is located
                 +
    HOW that state changes through time


9. PARALLEL BRANCH - DETECT NATURAL ACOUSTIC NODES
---------------------------------------------------

Node detection starts from the SAME 29 features, but it serves a different
purpose and therefore uses a separate normalization.

The trajectory branch asks:

    "Where do frames from all signals lie in a common acoustic space?"

The node branch asks:

    "Where does THIS signal change substantially relative to itself?"


The node pipeline is:

    +-----------------------+
    | ONE WAV: T x 29       |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | WITHIN-WAV            |
    | ROBUST SCALING        |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | DIFFERENCE BETWEEN    |
    | ADJACENT FRAMES       |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | EUCLIDEAN DISTANCE    |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | NOVELTY CURVE         |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | SMOOTH + FIND PEAKS   |
    +-----------------------+
               |
               v
    +-----------------------+
    | ACOUSTIC NODES        |
    +-----------------------+


For one WAV, the 29 features are robust-scaled independently.

The change between adjacent frames is then:

    novelty(t) =
        sqrt(
            SUM_(j=1)^29
            [Z_j(t) - Z_j(t-1)]^2
        )

Thus, novelty measures the total multivariate acoustic change from one frame
to the next.

A peak can therefore result from a change in pitch, spectral envelope,
amplitude, spectral distribution, or several properties simultaneously.

The novelty curve is smoothed over approximately 80 ms, and candidate peaks
are constrained by a minimum separation of approximately 120 ms.

The current implementation retains a maximum of 40 nodes.

The resulting nodes are therefore acoustic-change points rather than arbitrary
equal-duration divisions of the signal.


10. THE TWO BRANCHES REJOIN
---------------------------

The important distinction is:

                    SAME 29 FEATURES
                          |
             +------------+------------+
             |                         |
             v                         v
      SHARED NORMALIZATION      WITHIN-WAV NORMALIZATION
      across all WAVs           separately for each WAV
             |                         |
             v                         v
         PCA -> UMAP              novelty curve
             |                         |
             v                         v
           XYZ                    event nodes
             |                         |
             +------------+------------+
                          |
                          v
                TRAJECTORY + EVENTS


The shared branch determines the geometry in which signals can be compared.

The within-WAV branch determines where meaningful acoustic changes occur
along each individual signal.

These operations should not be confused: the node detector does not define
the XYZ coordinate system, and the shared UMAP projection does not determine
the node boundaries.

------------------------------------

    AUDIO
      |
      v
    STFT
      |
      v
    29 FEATURES / FRAME
      |
      +-----------------------------+
      |                             |
      | SHARED TRAJECTORY           | EVENT DETECTION
      |                             |
      v                             v
    pool all WAVs                one WAV
      |                             |
      v                             v
    RobustScaler                RobustScaler
      |                             |
      v                             v
    PCA <=10                   frame differences
      |                             |
      v                             v
    RobustScaler                Euclidean norm
      |                             |
      v                             v
    UMAP -> XYZ                novelty curve
      |                             |
      v                             v
    XYZ smoothing              smooth + peaks
      |                             |
      v                             v
    TRAJECTORY                    NODES
      |                             |
      +-------------+---------------+
                    |
                    v
          TRAJECTORY WITH
          ACOUSTIC EVENTS


12. CORE PRINCIPLE
------------------

The pipeline does not compare vocalizations using one acoustic measurement.

Instead, every short frame is first represented by 29 complementary acoustic
dimensions. These dimensions are placed onto comparable scales, compressed,
and projected into one shared three-dimensional acoustic space.

The chronological movement of a signal through that space forms its acoustic
trajectory.

Separately, changes across the same 29-dimensional representation are used to
identify natural within-signal acoustic events.

In short:

    29 acoustic features
            +
    common coordinate system
            +
    temporal order
            =
    acoustic trajectory

while:

    29 acoustic features
            +
    within-signal change
            =
    acoustic nodes
